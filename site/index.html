The problem

Agents pay before they know if they got what they paid for.

Payments are final.

An x402 payment settles before the response arrives. If the API fails or returns garbage, the protocol has no way back.

x402 · pay first, receive second

Escrow only moves the question.

Holding the funds is solved. Deciding whether the work was actually done isn't. Agent escrow tends to end at "an arbiter reviews", without naming one.

The missing piece is the judge

The buyer can judge their own case.

ERC-8183 lets the client appoint anyone as evaluator, themselves included. A buyer who judges their own purchase can reject good work and take the money back.

ERC-8183 · createJob(provider, evaluator, …)
How a ruling works

Commit first. Judge second. Sign everything.

Both sides lock in the terms before anyone knows the outcome. That's what makes the ruling hard to argue with.

  1. [1]

    Terms are committed

    The buyer states what "done" means as checks a machine can run: tests, a response schema, an on-chain condition.

    hash recorded when the job is created
  2. [2]

    Evidence is committed

    The seller delivers and commits a hash of exactly what they delivered, so the delivery can't be swapped mid-ruling.

    hash recorded at submit()
  3. [3]

    Verdict re-runs it

    We fetch both, check each against its hash, and re-run the checks in an isolated sandbox. The result is pass or fail, not an opinion.

    deterministic, reproducible
  4. [4]

    A signed ruling settles it

    The full ruling is published and signed. Its hash is recorded on-chain as the reason the escrow was released or refunded.

    complete() or reject()
Three outcomes, not two

When we can't verify, we don't guess.

Both sides rely on the arbiter, so one that guesses under uncertainty is worse than none. That's why abstaining is a ruling in its own right.

Passed

Every check passed.

The escrow is released to the seller. The ruling records each check, its output and how long it took.

Failed

The work arrived, and it's wrong.

The escrow is refused and the buyer can reclaim it. The ruling says exactly which checks failed and why.

Abstain

We couldn't verify it honestly.

We take no side and move no money. The escrow's own deadline returns the funds, and no judgement is passed.

What makes a judge worth trusting

Four rules we don't bend.

We never hold the money.

Funds stay in the escrow contract the parties chose. We issue rulings. We never hold, move or lend funds.

Same fee, whatever the outcome.

A ruling costs the same whether it passes, fails or abstains, so we have no reason to lean either way.

Every ruling can be re-run.

The terms and the evidence are both on-chain. Anyone can reproduce a ruling from chain data alone and check we got it right.

Abstain beats a guess.

If something doesn't check out, like a swapped file or a missing document, we refuse to rule rather than pick a side.

// The buyer states what "done" means, as checks a machine can run.
const terms = jobSpec({
  language: "python",
  tests: [
    { name: "adds two numbers",  stdin: "2 3",  expect: { stdout: "5" } },
    { name: "handles negatives", stdin: "-4 2", expect: { stdout: "-2" } },
  ],
});

// Put Verdict in the evaluator seat. Nobody else can release the escrow.
await commerce.write.createJob([
  provider,
  "0x668640c4f897F55661139D153Feee8b989d5d100", // Verdict
  deadline,
  encodeJobDescription({
    uri: dataUri(terms),        // terms live on-chain
    hash: hashDocument(terms),  // and are committed by hash
    summary: "sum two integers read from stdin",
  }),
  zeroAddress,
]);
Don't trust us. Check it.

Re-run any ruling from the chain alone.

No keys, no account, and no trust in our servers. The checker reads the job from Arc, rebuilds the terms and the evidence, re-runs the checks itself, and compares its answer with ours.

  • Terms and evidence match their on-chain hashes
  • An independent re-run reaches the same result
  • The ruling came from the named evaluator
  • The published ruling hashes to the on-chain reason
  • The signature recovers to Verdict's address
